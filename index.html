United Imaging Healthcare

Can high-end scanner wins translate into cash?

688271.SH / LONG / 12-MONTH VIEW

Alex Wu / 10 November 2026

Cover product image: United Imaging FY2025 annual report, uMR Astra.

英文演讲者备注与来源

0:00–1:00 | Opening

I would go long United Imaging Healthcare with a 12-month target of RMB144.45. That is approximately 23% price upside from the RMB117.55 Shanghai close on 10 November. The market already pays a premium for United Imaging. My case is that the company can earn that premium by turning more high-end scanner wins into installed systems, service contracts and cash.

There are two external forces in the case. AI changes the economics of running an imaging department and gives a challenger another way to compete. Geopolitics changes which tenders the company can bid on and how quickly overseas orders convert. I will start with those industry forces, then show how they flow through United Imaging’s business and the numbers I need to see to stay long.

1:00–4:00 | Industry

Start with demand. Imaging is a capital equipment market. Hospitals can defer purchases when budgets are tight, but the need to replace aging scanners and expand access does not go away. China’s equipment renewal program explicitly includes medical imaging and radiotherapy. County hospitals need basic capacity. Large tertiary hospitals have a different need: faster or higher-resolution machines for demanding clinical work. The distinction matters because policy can lift unit volumes while the higher-end mix determines how much gross profit vendors capture.

The chart tracks the share of domestic brands across high-end CT, MRI and PET/CT, from 25% in 2020 to 42% in 2025. This is a market estimate from Bocé Consulting via Huachuang. It shows local vendors gaining ground but says nothing about United Imaging’s individual share. Zhongtai projects a China imaging equipment market of roughly RMB108.5 billion in 2030, around 7% annual growth from 2022. I would treat that as a sizing estimate. In the first half of 2026 China imaging procurement contracted about 8% according to HSBC. That is why I separate the structural case from tender timing.

AI is commercially relevant where hospitals can measure the result. If software produces a diagnostic-quality image from a shorter acquisition, the same scanner can potentially see more patients in a day. Automation can also reduce setup variability across operators. The right diligence is to ask departments about room utilization and repeat scans. Incumbents offer similar capabilities, so I would look for a real workflow advantage in tenders and repeat orders.

Finally, geopolitics has different effects at home and abroad. European public tenders covered by the IPI can restrict Chinese bidders at €5 million or above. China has reciprocal restrictions on certain covered medical-device procurements at RMB45 million or above. Domestic production can matter under these rules, and each jurisdiction has exceptions. I would avoid treating the rules as either a blanket ban or a guaranteed domestic win. China is only about 13% of the global market in the cited estimate. The ability to deliver and service equipment across regions matters for the long-term opportunity.

4:00–7:00 | Company

United Imaging started in Shanghai in 2011. Its largest businesses are MRI and CT. Molecular imaging includes PET/CT and PET/MR. X-ray includes diagnostic systems and interventional DSA, which helps clinicians navigate blood vessels during procedures. Radiotherapy extends the offering into treatment. The five equipment lines account for RMB11.39 billion of FY25 revenue.

The advantage starts with component design. United Imaging can develop and manufacture core components, including high-field superconducting magnets, CT detectors and tubes. Control over the image chain allows it to tune hardware and software together, and can reduce reliance on externally sourced components. The practical proof is market share and repeat purchasing. The company reported the leading share of new equipment purchases in China in FY25 on a sales-value basis across its product lines excluding ultrasound. In the first nine months of 2026, management reported over 60% share in each of PET/CT and PET/MR. Those are modality-specific China figures, separate from the industry-wide localization chart.

Equipment is still more than four-fifths of revenue. About 62% of FY25 group revenue passed through distributors, and 38% was direct. A larger global installed base makes a new channel possible after installation: the customer can buy maintenance, spare parts and upgrades when the original warranty expires. Maintenance made roughly 62% gross margin in FY25, compared with roughly 47% for equipment. This is why I track the number of units installed alongside equipment orders.

There are tangible examples of premium equipment gaining traction overseas. The October call flagged advanced MRI installations at SingHealth in Singapore and a first uMI Panvivo PET/CT installation at a specialist imaging provider in Japan. These are proof points for clinical adoption. The investment question is whether a reference site can lead to repeat orders and paid service coverage.

7:00–11:00 | Financials

The top table makes the difference between a cyclical rebound and structural growth visible. Revenue fell 10% in FY24 and rebounded 34% in FY25. Overseas revenue grew through both years. In the first nine months of FY26 the group added about RMB1.72 billion of revenue, of which roughly RMB1 billion came from overseas. Overseas mix reached 28.3%, up 5.8 points year on year. Domestic growth improved from about 8.5% in the first half to about 15% in Q3. The company and Goldman’s breakdowns are directionally consistent.

Earnings were weaker than sales. Third-quarter revenue grew 24% and attributable profit grew 13%, but through nine months profit fell 7.6%. Consolidated gross margin was 46.2% in the first nine months, compared with 47.0% a year earlier. The standalone quarter ran at 44.3%. I would avoid inferring geographic net margins from the revenue data. On FY25 core business, China gross margin was 49.3% and overseas was 46.6%. Overseas scale helps diversify the business but does not mechanically lift gross margin.

The company reports both expensed R&D and total R&D investment, which includes capitalized development. Through nine months, expensed R&D reached 16.0% of sales, compared with 14.1% a year earlier. Total R&D investment was 21.1% of sales. Selling and G&A ratios fell against the comparable nine-month period. Goldman also points to the drag from share-based compensation and FX. The company reported profit before tax excluding FX up 7.2% through nine months. I would keep reported profit and cash flow as the decision metrics.

On mix, FY25 MRI and molecular imaging grew much faster than CT. In 9M26, 5T MRI sales increased more than 70% and radiotherapy doubled from a small base. Maintenance was up 21.5%, including overseas service growth of 60.9%. AI is already inside the product. For example, uMR Ultra uses the uAIFI.LIVE platform to reconstruct continuous motion images, while uMR Astra uses deep learning to accelerate scans. These features can help win higher-value tenders or justify paid upgrades. Standalone software revenue fell to RMB12.8 million in 1H26. That is total software revenue, so there is insufficient evidence to forecast a separate material AI software stream.

For the earnings base, I use Goldman’s post-Q3 forecast. It is more conservative than Huachuang’s July forecast for FY26 attributable profit, RMB1.98 billion versus RMB2.31 billion. Goldman requires a strong fourth quarter. The company must deliver about RMB6.16 billion of sales and RMB0.94 billion of attributable profit, which equates to roughly 25% and 26% growth respectively. I would focus on what is delivered and accepted and what actually gets collected.

11:00–15:00 | Recommendation, valuation and risks

My call is long at RMB117.55 with a twelve-month target of RMB144.45. The three legs are premium equipment share gains at home, overseas installations that can support paid maintenance, and AI features that increase the equipment’s value to hospitals. I would defend this as a conditional long. The growth headline alone is insufficient when group operating cash flow is negative through nine months.

The earnings anchor is RMB3.21 for FY27 from Goldman’s updated estimates. My 45 times multiple is a case assumption, which gives a RMB144.45 target. It requires the market to value FY27 EPS at 45 times versus about 36.6 times at the reference close, as margin visibility and collections improve. On FY28 EPS of RMB4.38, the same target is about 33 times. At the current FY27 multiple the base earnings estimate gives roughly the current price, so a rerating is essential to this call. Huachuang’s July RMB140 target and Goldman’s October RMB169 target are independent broker references with different methods. Huachuang used 50 times FY26 EPS. Goldman used a two-stage DCF with a 9% discount rate and 2% terminal growth. The pitch uses neither broker’s target as its own.

The comp set has three global operating peers and Mindray as a separate China-listed reference. UIH’s 36.6 times FY27 EPS is already above the global median of 20 times. I would argue for a premium based on a 34% FY27 earnings rebound in the forecast and the scope for service growth, while acknowledging the growth rate partly reflects a weak FY26 comparison. I would avoid using peers’ trading multiples mechanically. Philips has a broader business mix, and Siemens closes its fiscal year in September. A bear case with FY27 EPS of RMB2.65 and a 35 times multiple gives RMB92.75, about 21% downside. This is a scenario, not a claim about the probability of that outcome.

What changes my view? First, whether the fourth quarter meets the delivery hurdle and the full year returns to positive operating cash flow. Second, whether overseas reference installations turn into repeated orders and paid service. Third, whether operating leverage arrives without a deterioration in tender prices or gross margin. I would follow EU tender eligibility and FX. I would also check that inventories are turning into recognized sales and cash, given the 43% increase since year-end. Persistent weak cash conversion would make a 45 times target hard to defend.

Close with the decision. United Imaging has credible proof that its equipment can compete. I would own it for the earnings recovery only while the evidence shows that installed systems are generating cash.
